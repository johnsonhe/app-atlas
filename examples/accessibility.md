# Accessibility Analysis of the Mendeley Pooled Review Corpus

An analysis of the unattributed Mendeley pooled review corpus reveals distinct user experiences and quantitative trends regarding screen readers, text size, and contrast. [S1](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S1)

While visual accessibility issues like small text and low contrast are frequently reported, screen reader compatibility remains a critical area of concern for visually impaired users. ## Quantitative Keyword Match Analysis

Within the unattributed Mendeley pooled corpus of 155,933 reviews, keyword searches identify varying levels of discussion around accessibility features. [S1](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S1)

The term 'screen reader' matched 6,934 reviews, representing a 4.45% keyword-match share. [S5](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S5)

Visual accessibility terms show notable volumes, with 'too small' matching 9,852 reviews (6.32% share) and 'hard to read' matching 8,578 reviews (5.50% share). [S35](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S35) [S36](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S36)

More specific visual terms appear less frequently, with 'text size' matching 3,039 reviews (1.95% share), 'font size' matching 891 reviews (0.57% share), and 'contrast' matching 36 reviews (0.02% share). [S4](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S4) [S7](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S7) [S8](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S8)

Dedicated screen reader terms yield smaller counts, with 'VoiceOver' matching 114 reviews (0.07% share) and 'talkback' returning 0 matches. [S2](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S2) [S9](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S9)

## User-Reported Accessibility Barriers

Users highlight severe visual barriers, such as microscopic fonts and low-contrast color pairings like orange text on a light orange background. [S28](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S28)

Cluttered homepages with poor contrast and small icons make it difficult for users to quickly distinguish and press interactive buttons. [S27](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S27)

Screen reader users report critical regressions where updates render previously selectable text unreadable to assistive technologies. [S17](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S17)

## Positive and Contradictory Experiences

In contrast to negative reports, some users explicitly praise apps for being fully accessible with the VoiceOver screen reader. [S25](/api/reports/c7fa2a0d11b5440cb4fb07b93c34e7e1/sources/S25)

## What the data cannot establish

- The Mendeley corpus lacks app identifiers, categories, dates, and storefront metadata, preventing any app-specific or category-specific comparisons.
- Keyword matches reflect simple token presence and do not establish semantic intent or confirm that every matched review represents an actual complaint.
- The dataset is a static snapshot and does not represent the entire App Store or real-time user experiences.

## Questions to investigate next

- Conduct a systematic WCAG compliance audit on apps with high volumes of 'hard to read' or 'contrast' keywords to map user complaints to objective design failures.
- Investigate the impact of Dynamic Type integration on user retention by analyzing rating distributions before and after accessibility updates.
- Perform usability testing with screen reader users to identify common navigation blocks and unlabeled interactive elements in mobile layouts.
